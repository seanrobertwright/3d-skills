B30–P30 Seam Survey

Seven stations, in order. Each one shows where the tool goes and which number to write down. Type the readings here and press Save; the design is built from this sheet, not from the spec pages.

CaliperDigital, 150 mm. Write down exactly what the display shows; do not round.
Steel rule150 mm or longer, truly straight. Its length gets written down once.
Feeler gaugesBest. Without them: ordinary printer paper is about 0.1 mm a sheet. Stack sheets until the stack just will not slide in, and count them.
Pencil and masking tapeFor marking 16 mm, 36 mm and 60 mm on the backs.
1IDENTIFY

Which monitor is which

"B30" and "P30" are the names the monitors report to Windows, not Sceptre part numbers. Two things to settle: which physical screen Windows calls B30, and whether the refresh rates match the research (B30 at 100 Hz, P30 at 200 Hz).

  1. Windows: Settings → System → Display. Press Identify: a number appears on each screen. Note the number on the left screen.
  2. Open Advanced display. In the dropdown at the top, pick that number and read the name beside it. That is the left screen's name.
  3. For each display in the dropdown, open Choose a refresh rate and write down the highest number offered.

Now open the seam

Stations 2 to 5 need the inner edges reachable and the backs reachable. Slide the monitors at least 100 mm apart, and pull them far enough from the wall that you can get a hand and a caliper behind each inner edge. They get butted again for stations 6 and 7.

2RIM

The front rim, and the dark border

The B30 is reported to have a thin plastic rim standing proud of the glass at the edge; the P30 is reported edgeless. Measure both; 0 is the expected answer on one of them. The rim height decides how thick the shim in station 4 must be, and the border width is for an optional strip that hides the seam.

  1. About 160 mm up, run a fingernail from the glass out over the edge. If it catches on nothing, the rim is flush: write 0 and skip to step 4.
  2. Lay feeler blades flat on the glass, pushed up against the inboard face of the rim. Close the caliper and lay its jaws across the rim and the stack.
  3. Add or remove blades until the jaws sit on both without rocking. Add up the stack: that is the rim height. Paper works the same way: count the sheets.
  4. Show a plain white image full-screen. With the rule or the caliper flat on the glass, measure from the housing's end face to the first lit pixel.
  5. Repeat on the other monitor.

3REAR FIELD

Where the back becomes flat, and whether it stays flat

The bridge's pads bond to the back between 16 and 56 mm in from the inner edge. They need a smooth surface there, and the pad has to start past the chamfer or rounded corner at the edge. This station also lays down the pencil marks that station 4 uses.

  1. Stick a strip of masking tape on the back running inboard from the inner edge at 160 mm up. With the rule, pencil marks on it at 16, 36 and 60 mm from the housing's end face.
  2. Hold the rule on edge along the back, its end overhanging the edge and its 60 mm point on the 60 mark. Press it down at the 36 mark.
  3. Where does the rule first touch the back? Measure from the rear corner of the end face to that point: FACET. A fingernail run across the corner finds the same place.
  4. Still pressing at 36, find the thickest feeler (or paper stack) that slides under the rule at the 16 mark, then at 56 mm. Write both; 0 means it touches.
  5. Slide the rule up and down the pad zone (100 to 220 mm up). If anywhere it rocks, or a gap jumps, describe where in the note.
  6. Repeat on the other monitor, including the tape and marks.

4THICKNESS

Front-to-back thickness at the inner edge

The most important numbers on the sheet. The bridge holds the two front faces flush by making up the difference in the two housings' thickness, so each is needed to a few hundredths, at 16 mm in where the pad starts, and again at 36 mm in so the slope of the back is known. Both readings are taken from the glass, never from the rim.

Two things to get right. The fixed jaw rests on the shim on the glass, never on the rim; the sliding jaw goes on the back with its tip on the mark. Close gently until both just touch, then check the caliper does not rock.
  1. Pick a shim: one feeler blade thicker than the rim (0.5 mm is usually right; if the rim is flush, any blade or none). Measure it with the caliper and write it down: SHIM. Without feelers, a 12 mm-wide strip cut from a plastic card, measured the same way.
  2. Lay the shim flat on the glass, running up-and-down, directly opposite the 16 mark.
  3. Open the caliper, bring it over the edge with the fixed jaw on the shim and the sliding jaw behind, and close until the jaw tip meets the back on the 16 mark. Write what the display shows: T16_160.
  4. Open, lift off, and do it again from scratch: T16_160_B. If the two differ by more than 0.05 mm, take a third and put it in the note.
  5. Same at 100 mm up and 220 mm up, with the shim moved to match: T16_100, T16_220. No tape needed there: 16 mm in by eye against the rule is fine.
  6. Back at 160 mm up, move the shim opposite the 36 mark and close with the tip on the 36 mark: T36_160. If the jaws cannot reach 36, use the deepest mark they can and write that depth.
  7. Repeat all of it on the other monitor.

5KEEP-OUT

What is on the back where the pads go

The pads sit 16 to 56 mm in from each inner edge and 100 to 220 mm up; the spine behind them reaches 60 mm in and stands 14 mm proud. The B30's rear buttons live at its lower inner corner, and both backs have raised wings, lights and ports somewhere. While you are back there, read the model labels too.

  1. On each back, find the pad zone: 16 to 56 mm in from the inner edge, 100 to 220 mm up. Is it plain cover: nothing raised, recessed, vented or lit, and no cable plugged in there?
  2. On both, measure from the inner edge to the nearest edge of the nearest raised feature anywhere between 100 and 220 mm up: a wing, a light strip, a boss, a step.
  3. On the B30, measure from the bottom edge up to the top of the button cluster, and from the inner edge in to the nearest edge of the nearest button. If the buttons sit on a raised boss, measure to the boss.
  4. Look at each inner END face, the flat side that faces the seam: plain, or is there a lip, a button, a port?
  5. Ports: measure from the inner edge to the nearest port or cable exit between 100 and 220 mm up. Leave blank if there is none within 80 mm.
  6. Photograph the rear label of each and copy the model number.

Now butt them again

Tilt both screens all the way back to the stop (or both all the way forward: the same end on both). Slide them together until something touches, then ease them apart to the smallest gap the stands allow. Leave them there for stations 6 and 7.

6SEAM

Does the glass run through the seam as one curve

The bridge is designed for both screens on one 1800 mm circle. A steel rule laid flat across the glass tells the difference between one curve, a toe-in and a flat butt, and it does not care what shape the housings' end faces are. This station also records the gap the stands allow and the gap you want.

  1. With the monitors butted, measure the gap between the two front faces at the glass, and again about 15 mm behind the glass on the flat part of the end faces. Note what stopped them getting closer.
  2. Lay the rule flat on the glass of one monitor, 160 mm up, well away from the seam. Find the thickest feeler or paper stack that fits under its middle: MIDGAP_ONE.
  3. Lay the same rule across the seam, centred on it, 160 mm up. Does it sit on both ends, or rock on the seam? If it sits, find the middle gap: MIDGAP_SEAM. If it rocks, write 0 and say so.
  4. Say how you want them: one continuous curve (what this design assumes) or each turned in toward you. And how close: touching, 2 mm, or wider.

7LEVEL

Are the tops level with each other

The bridge cannot lift one screen; it can only tolerate a small height offset. Sceptre publishes the same with-stand height for both to 0.1 mm, but that is a spec, not your desk.

  1. Lay the rule across both top edges, centred on the seam.
  2. The rule rests on the higher one. Find the thickest feeler or paper stack that slides under it above the lower one, right next to the seam.
  3. If the rule touches both, write 0 and "neither".
